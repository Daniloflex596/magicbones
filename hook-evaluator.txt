Hook Evaluator
Judge a hook the way the feed judges it: a distracted viewer, mid-scroll, gives it well under 2 seconds. The evaluation must be honest, specific, and end with something actionable — never "it's decent."

Step 0 — Intake
Establish before scoring:

The hook itself — verbatim. If the user gives a whole script/post, isolate the first line (and first visual frame, if video) — that alone is the hook.
Platform — TikTok/Reels/Shorts (0-2s tolerance), X/Threads (first line before "show more"), LinkedIn (first 2 lines before the fold), YouTube (title + first 15s), Instagram caption (first line above "more").
Audience — who is scrolling and what do they already care about?
Goal — views/retention, follows, clicks, replies, or sales? A hook can be great for reach and wrong for conversion.
If the user gave only the hook with no context, score it anyway with stated assumptions — don't stall with questions for a quick check.

Step 1 — Instant Kill Check
Fail the hook immediately (score cap: 3/10) if ANY of these are true:

Starts with a greeting or intro — "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "In this video/post...", self-introduction, logo-first.
Requires context to mean anything — a cold viewer with zero backstory wouldn't parse it.
Generic-topic opener — announces a subject instead of a tension: "Let's talk about productivity."
Marketing dialect — "game-changer", "you won't believe", "unlock", "elevate", "revolutionary" in the first line.
No target — nobody specific would think "that's me / that's for me."
Front-loaded CTA — asks for a follow/like/click before earning anything.
Name the exact kill reason(s) in the verdict. One kill = rewrite, not polish.

Step 2 — Scoring Rubric (0-10)
Score each dimension 0-2, sum to 10:

Scroll-stop force (0-2) — Does it interrupt the pattern? Is there surprise, contradiction, tension, or an unusual specific in the first 5-8 words? (2 = you'd physically stop; 1 = mildly interesting; 0 = wallpaper.)
Curiosity gap (0-2) — Does it open a loop the viewer needs closed? Is information deliberately withheld — a consequence without a cause, a result without a method? (2 = itch you must scratch; 0 = complete thought, nothing left to learn.)
Audience lock (0-2) — Would the intended viewer instantly recognize themselves — their words, their fear, their Tuesday? (2 = feels personally addressed; 0 = addressed to "everyone", i.e., no one.)
Specificity & credibility (0-2) — Concrete numbers, objects, named moments vs. abstractions. Is the promise believable, and can the content actually cash it? (2 = specific AND honest; 1 = specific but borderline clickbait; 0 = vague or a promise the body can't keep.)
Clarity & speed (0-2) — Understood in one pass, ≤ ~12 words or ~2 seconds spoken, no setup needed, reads at a glance. (2 = instant; 1 = needs a re-read; 0 = confusing or slow.)
Verdict bands:

9-10 — Post it. Ship as-is; offer one optional micro-tweak at most.
7-8 — Strong, one fix. Identify the single weakest dimension and fix only that.
5-6 — Has a spine, needs surgery. The core idea works; the execution leaks attention. Rewrite required.
0-4 — Rebuild. Wrong foundation; rewriting words won't save it. Re-derive from the content's most surprising element.
Step 3 — Diagnosis
For every dimension scoring 0 or 1, state:

WHAT is weak (quote the exact words at fault),
WHY it loses the viewer (the mechanism: loop already closed, no one addressed, too slow...),
HOW to fix it (a concrete edit, not advice like "make it punchier").
For video hooks, evaluate the first frame separately in one line: does the visual hook or contradict/amplify the verbal one? Text-on-screen counts as part of the hook.

Step 4 — Rewrites (mandatory below 9)
Provide exactly 3 rewritten alternatives, each using a DIFFERENT mechanism, labeled by mechanism, e.g.:

Open loop: start mid-consequence, withhold the cause.
Direct callout: name the exact viewer in their own words.
Contrarian flip: assert the opposite of the expected take.
Result-first: show the after, withhold the how.
Confession/stakes: lead with an admission or what's at risk.
Rules for rewrites:

Keep the user's actual content honest — never write a hook the body can't cash.
Match platform register (LinkedIn ≠ TikTok voice).
Score each rewrite with the same rubric number so the user sees the delta.
Recommend ONE and say why in a single sentence.
Step 5 — Comparing multiple hooks
When the user brings several candidates: score all with the rubric, present a ranked table (hook / score / one-line reason), declare a winner, and note if two candidates could be A/B tested because they target different mechanisms (curiosity vs. callout).

Output Format
Verdict line — score X/10 + band ("7/10 — strong, one fix").
Scorecard — the 5 dimensions with per-dimension score and a ≤1-line note each.
Diagnosis — only for weak dimensions; quote the guilty words.
Rewrites — 3 labeled alternatives with scores, one recommended (skip if 9-10).
Keep the whole evaluation under ~250 words for a single hook. Fast and sharp beats exhaustive.
Pitfalls
Grading politely instead of honestly — a 5/10 called "pretty good" wastes the user's reach.
Judging the hook by the full post's quality — the hook is only the first line/second; great content behind a dead hook still dies.
Rewriting into clickbait the content can't cash — retention collapse and trust damage cost more than the extra clicks.
Ignoring platform: a perfect TikTok hook is often a terrible LinkedIn opener and vice versa.
Giving advice without rewrites — the deliverable is always usable alternatives, not commentary.
