Format Selection Guide
Before writing anything, select the right format. Wrong format choice is the most common reason a marketing video underperforms. Match the product type and goal to the format.

UGC Best for: consumer goods, supplements, skincare, apparel, apps, gadgets. Responds to: casual first-person language, personal story framing, authentic reactions. Sweet spot: products where social proof and relatable real-person endorsement drives conversion. Avoid: premium luxury goods — UGC energy works against high-ticket positioning.

TV Spot Best for: brand campaigns, product launches, established brands, high-ticket items. Responds to: cinematic language, story arc, emotional narrative, polished VO. Sweet spot: any product that benefits from a brand-first rather than product-first treatment. Avoid: direct response or impulse-buy products — TV Spot is brand-building, not conversion-first.

Tutorial Best for: apps, SaaS, tools, kitchen products, beauty devices, anything with a learning curve. Responds to: step-by-step casual delivery, screen-adjacent visuals, how-to framing. Sweet spot: products where showing how it works is the most persuasive thing you can do. Avoid: simple commodities — if there's nothing to demonstrate, Tutorial adds no value.

Product Review Best for: electronics, gear, tools, gadgets, any product where features and quality matter. Responds to: hands-on close-up footage, voiceover evaluation tone, key spec callouts. Sweet spot: considered purchases where the buyer wants to see the product in use before buying. Avoid: impulse or fashion items — reviews slow the pace when speed of desire matters.

Unboxing Best for: gifting occasions, subscription boxes, premium packaging, tech, sneakers. Responds to: first-touch energy, tactile reveals, authentic reaction to packaging quality. Sweet spot: products where the packaging and reveal experience is part of the value. Avoid: digital products or anything where there is nothing physical to unwrap.

UGC Virtual Try-On Best for: apparel, accessories, eyewear, footwear, hats, anything worn on the body. Responds to: casual home try-on energy, pose/reaction shots, fit commentary. Sweet spot: fashion and accessories where seeing it worn casually on a real person matters. Avoid: non-wearable products — forced try-on framing looks absurd.

Hyper Motion Best for: beverages, perfume, luxury goods, tech hardware, beauty products. Responds to: CGI-level product description, physics language (liquid pour, condensation, surface reflection), premium set design. Sweet spot: any product that should be the hero of the frame with no human presence. Avoid: products that need a person to contextualise their use.

Pro Virtual Try-On Best for: premium fashion, designer accessories, activewear, high-end streetwear. Responds to: editorial photography language, outdoor/urban environments, cinematic scale. Sweet spot: fashion that benefits from a magazine-editorial look rather than a home try-on. Avoid: budget or fast-fashion products — Pro Try-On elevates positioning, which needs to match price.

Wild Card Best for: any product, any goal — highest creative freedom. Responds to: scenario-driven descriptions, character and location prompts, narrative arcs. Sweet spot: brands that want unexpected creative or are testing bold concepts. Avoid: performance marketers who need predictable format compliance — Wild Card output varies.

Use a library avatar when:

Hook Engineering
The hook is the first 2–3 seconds. Write it before the script body.

Hook frameworks
Problem-first hook Open with the pain before the product. "If you're still [doing X the old way], you're wasting [time/money/energy]." Best for: UGC, Tutorial, Product Review.

Pattern interrupt hook Start with something visually or verbally unexpected. "Wait — don't scroll past this." Best for: UGC, Wild Card.

Bold claim hook Lead with a specific, falsifiable statement. "This [product] [did measurable thing] in [short time period]." Best for: UGC, Product Review.

Curiosity gap hook State something incomplete that requires watching to resolve. "I switched from [X] to this and I'm never going back — here's why." Best for: UGC, Tutorial.

Social proof hook Open with scale or endorsement. "[Number] people switched to this in [timeframe]." Best for: TV Spot, Product Review.

Transformation tease hook Show or imply the after-state before explaining the before. "This is what [timeframe] looks like." Best for: Fitness, Skincare, Unboxing.

Hook length by platform
TikTok: hook must land in first 1 second. 3–5 words max on screen.
Instagram Reels: 2 seconds. First VO line must create immediate tension.
YouTube Shorts: 3 seconds. More information density allowed.
Script Architecture
Every Marketing Studio video follows a 4-part structure:

1. Hook (0–3s) — Stop the scroll. One statement. No brand name yet. 2. Problem or Context (3–10s) — Name the viewer's situation without naming the product. 3. Product + Proof (10–25s) — Introduce product. Show it working. Include one specific proof point. 4. CTA (25–30s) — Single directional verb. One CTA only.

Script length by platform
TikTok: 15–30 seconds. Every word earns its place.
Instagram Reels: 15–45 seconds. Slightly more story-build allowed.
YouTube Shorts: up to 60 seconds. Strongest tolerance for detail.

Common Mistakes to Fix
Problem	Likely cause	Fix
Ad feels generic	Hook is a benefit statement, not a pattern interrupt	Rewrite hook using problem-first or curiosity gap framework
No one watches past 3 seconds	Hook too slow or too soft	Lead with the most provocative sentence — move exposition to seconds 5–10
Weak CTA	Vague ("learn more", "check it out")	Replace with directional verb + specific action ("tap the link to get 20% off today")
Avatar feels mismatched	Demographic mismatch	Reselect avatar by matching target buyer's age, gender, and lifestyle energy
Script too long	Nothing cut	Rule: if removing a sentence doesn't lose a proof point or CTA, cut it
Wrong format for product	Format chosen by preference not product type	Luxury → TV Spot or Hyper Motion; social proof goods → UGC
Hyper Motion looks fake	Abstract language instead of physics descriptors	Add: condensation, liquid viscosity, surface reflection, material-specific language
UGC feels scripted	Language too polished	Rewrite in first-person casual with contractions and natural hesitations
Product not visible	No explicit placement direction	State: "product held in right hand at chest height, label facing camera"
