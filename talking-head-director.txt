Avatar Selection Guide
Before writing a single word of script, select the avatar. Avatar choice determines the script's tone, pacing, vocabulary level, and credibility register.

Avatar archetypes and when to use each
Expert / Authority Visual: professional presentation, clean grooming, neutral or dark tones, direct eye contact. Use for: educational content, thought leadership, how-to explainers, coaching, B2B. Script tone: measured, confident, clear. No filler words. Sentences complete. Avoid: slang, casual contractions, overly enthusiastic delivery.

Relatable Creator Visual: casual styling, warm or lifestyle backdrop, natural expression range. Use for: UGC-adjacent content, product recommendations, personal story, social proof. Script tone: first-person, casual, contractions, natural hesitations acceptable. Avoid: corporate language, formal sentence structure, anything that sounds scripted.

Brand Character / Soul ID Visual: trained Soul character matching brand identity. Use for: brand campaigns, AI influencer content, series with character continuity. Script tone: defined by the character's persona — set before scripting. Avoid: tone that contradicts the character bible.

Neutral Presenter Visual: clean, minimal, professional but not formal. Use for: product demos, app walkthroughs, announcements, multi-demographic content. Script tone: clear and benefit-focused. Neither corporate nor casual. Avoid: strong personality — neutral presenters should not have opinions.

Script Architecture
Every talking head video follows a 4-part structure. Write for the ear, not the eye — test every sentence by speaking it aloud.

1. Open (0–5s) Start with the viewer's world. Never open with the brand name, speaker name, or generic welcome.

Strong opens:

Problem-first: "Most people are doing [X] wrong — and it's costing them [consequence]."
Bold claim: "[Specific thing] changed my [outcome] in [timeframe]."
Right question: "Have you ever wondered why [common frustration]?"
Weak opens (avoid): "Hey guys, welcome back to..." / "Today I want to talk about..." / "Hi, I'm [name]..."

2. Body (5s to [duration minus 10s]) One idea per sentence. Maximum 2 ideas per paragraph.

Structure by content type:

Explainer / Educational: Problem → Cause → Solution → Proof
Product demo: Context → Feature → Benefit → Proof
Thought leadership: Claim → Evidence → Implication → Call to think
Personal / Storytelling: Scene → Tension → Turn → Takeaway
3. Transition to CTA (last 5–8s of body) Consequence bridge: "Which means if you [don't act / do act], you [negative / positive outcome]."

4. CTA (final 5s) One action. Directional verb. Specific destination.

Strong: "Tap the link in my bio to start your free trial."
Weak: "Let me know in the comments what you think."
Teleprompter Script Formatting
Scripts for AI avatars must be formatted for on-camera delivery, not reading.

Sentence length: Maximum 15 words per sentence. Split anything longer.

Delivery markers:

[PAUSE] — half-second beat after a strong statement
[EMPHASIS] — stress the next word or phrase
[SLOWER] — reduce pace for complex or important information
[FASTER] — increase energy for lists or momentum moments
Paragraph breaks = breath breaks. 2–3 sentences maximum per paragraph.

Write out all numbers and symbols in full:

$47 → forty-seven dollars
3x → three times
#1 → number one
Avoid: Parenthetical asides, em dashes within sentences, ellipses — these confuse lipsync pacing.

Common Mistakes to Fix
Problem	Likely cause	Fix
Avatar sounds robotic	Script written for reading, not speaking	Read every sentence aloud; split any sentence over 15 words
Lipsync feels off	Numbers or symbols in raw form	Write out all numbers, symbols, and abbreviations in full spoken form
Nobody watches past 5 seconds	Opening starts with speaker or brand	Rewrite open to start with viewer's problem or a bold claim
Video feels too long	Every idea included, nothing cut	If removing a sentence doesn't remove a proof point or CTA, cut it
CTA has no result	Soft or vague CTA	Directional verb + specific destination or outcome
Wrong avatar for the content	Avatar chosen by aesthetics not function	Expertise content → Expert/Authority; personal story → Relatable Creator
Script works on one platform but not another	Delivery not calibrated per platform	Re-run Platform Delivery Guide; adjust pacing, energy, and duration
Workflow
Identify platform and goal — where will this run; what should the viewer do after watching?
Select avatar archetype — match to platform and content type
Determine duration — set the maximum before writing
Write the open first — the first sentence is the most important; don't proceed until it's right
Build the body — choose the structure that fits the content type
Write the CTA — one action, one directional verb, one destination
Format for teleprompter — apply sentence length, delivery markers, paragraph breaks
Output the Talking Head Brief — full structured brief with scene direction and two variations
When improving a script that isn't performing: identify the exact sentence where a viewer would stop watching, diagnose why, and rewrite from that point. Do not rewrite the whole script if only one section is broken.
